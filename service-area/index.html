Plumbing Service Area: Pompano Beach & Nearby Cities

Pompano Beach 24/7 Plumbing is based in Pompano Beach and dispatches into the neighboring coastal cities below. Housing age and pipe material vary by city here, so we cover each one on its own page.

Cities We Cover

Pipe age and construction era shift noticeably across this part of Broward County. A canal-front home in Lighthouse Point built in the 1960s has little in common, plumbing-wise, with a 1990s-built house in Coconut Creek, even though both are a short drive from our Pompano Beach base. Each city page below covers what's actually common in that specific area rather than treating the whole region as one market.

Coverage isn't limited strictly to city lines. If an address falls between two of the areas listed, call (954) 697-4920 and we'll confirm dispatch rather than turn it down on a technicality.

Here's the short version of what's different about each city, with the full picture on each one's own page. Pompano Beach itself, home base for dispatch, has the mid-century cast-iron signature this whole network page has already covered. Lighthouse Point is almost entirely canal-front construction from the 1950s-70s boom, which brings dock and outdoor plumbing into the picture alongside the pipe age. Deerfield Beach splits into an older beachside core and a somewhat newer inland side, so which ZIP an address falls in actually matters there. Margate is inland and built in one concentrated stretch in the early 1970s, including a large 55-plus community, which makes galvanized pipe age the dominant factor. Coconut Creek spans a wider construction era than its neighbors, from 1970s sections through real growth after 2000, so its plumbing calls don't follow one single pattern the way some of the other cities do. Oakland Park is the oldest city in this coverage area, with pockets of housing from the 1950s near Middle River.

None of that changes how a call gets handled. Dispatch works the same way sitewide: call, get matched with the nearest available technician, and get pricing confirmed before any repair starts, regardless of which city the address is in.

Coverage decisions come down to distance from the Pompano Beach dispatch base and whether a technician is realistically available within a reasonable window, not a rigid map boundary. An address a few blocks past one of the five cities listed here is still very likely coverable, which is exactly why we'd rather confirm on a quick call than have someone rule themselves out based on a city name alone.

Each city's own page goes into more depth than this summary can: real ZIP codes, the housing eras that actually matter for plumbing, named subdivisions, and what a technician typically expects walking in. That level of detail is deliberate. Two cities that are both "nearby Pompano Beach" on a map can have almost nothing in common once you look at when they were built and what's actually running through the walls.

This isn't the full extent of where a call might get answered, either. Addresses just outside these five cities, in unincorporated parts of the county or smaller pockets between them, get the same treatment on the phone: a straightforward confirmation of whether dispatch reaches that specific address, rather than a hard no based on a city boundary that doesn't reflect how close a technician actually is.

Housing age is the thread running through every one of these city pages, and it's worth restating plainly here: this whole stretch of coastal Broward County built up in roughly the same 30-year window, but not all at once and not all with the same materials. A canal-front lot dug in 1958 and a subdivision built in 1998 can sit five miles apart and share almost nothing plumbing-wise, which is exactly why treating "the area" as one uniform market misses what actually predicts a service call.

If none of the five cities listed matches an address, that doesn't automatically mean dispatch can't help. Call and give the city or ZIP directly, and we'll say plainly and honestly whether it's inside the coverage area rather than leave that unclear.

Each of the five city pages below covers real ZIP codes, named subdivisions, and the specific plumbing pattern most common there, built from actual research rather than a generic template with the city name swapped in each time. That's a deliberate standard we hold every page in this network to, not just the ones a visitor happens to read first.
