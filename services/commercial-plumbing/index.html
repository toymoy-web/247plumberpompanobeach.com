How commercial calls differ from residential ones

A commercial property usually has higher fixture counts, grease-heavy kitchen drains, backflow prevention devices that need periodic testing, and sometimes a property manager or multiple decision-makers involved before work can start. We ask about all of this upfront, since a restaurant kitchen line and a retail restroom are different jobs even when the underlying plumbing issue looks similar on paper.

Scheduling is the other real difference. A grease trap cleaning or drain jetting job at a restaurant works better before opening or after close than in the middle of lunch service, and we build the appointment around that whenever the job allows it.

What we handle for commercial and multi-unit properties

Grease trap cleaning, backflow testing, commercial drain and sewer line service, water heater service for larger commercial units, and general plumbing repair for offices and retail space all fall under this umbrella. For multi-unit residential buildings, we also handle shared-line issues that a single-unit call wouldn't cover, coordinating with building management when a shared stack is involved.

Backflow prevention and permits

Most commercial properties in Florida are required to have a tested backflow prevention device, which keeps contaminated water from siphoning back into the public supply. Testing is typically required annually, and we handle both the testing and any repair the device needs to pass, along with the paperwork the water utility requires afterward.

For a property undergoing renovation or a new commercial buildout, plumbing permits and inspections are part of the process, and we coordinate directly with the permitting office rather than leaving that step to the property owner or general contractor to sort out separately.

What to do before help arrives at a commercial property

For an active leak or flooding at a business, shut off the main water valve if it's accessible and safe to reach, and avoid running electrical equipment near standing water. Move inventory or equipment off a wet floor where possible, and document the affected area with photos for insurance purposes before cleanup begins.

Emergency commercial calls

A burst pipe, sewage backup, or failed water heater at a business doesn't wait for a convenient time, and commercial emergency calls get the same 24/7 dispatch as residential ones. The difference is scope: a restaurant with no working water can't operate at all, so we prioritize getting water service restored even on a temporary basis while a fuller repair gets scheduled.

For a property with a service contract already in place, emergency calls move faster since the technician arrives already familiar with the building's plumbing layout and any past issues on file, rather than starting from zero on an unfamiliar system.

Pricing

What Actually Affects Cost

  • Property size and fixture count
  • Scheduling around business hours vs after-hours rates
  • Scope (single repair vs ongoing service agreement)
  • Permit and inspection coordination for larger jobs
  • Multi-tenant coordination where applicable

Most homeowner insurance policies cover a sudden, accidental plumbing failure, a burst pipe, for example, but not gradual wear or a line that failed from ordinary age-related corrosion. That's the honest baseline to plan around rather than assume coverage applies.

FAQ

Commercial Plumbing Questions

Can you schedule around our business hours?

Yes, for planned work like grease trap cleaning or backflow testing, we schedule around opening and closing whenever the job allows it. Emergency calls still get same-day dispatch.

Do you work with property management companies?

Yes, including coordinating access, invoicing details, and multi-unit shared-line issues that involve more than one tenant.

Do you test backflow prevention devices?

Yes, backflow testing is part of standard commercial service, required periodically on most commercial properties in Florida.

Do you provide documentation for health department inspections?

Yes, records of backflow testing, grease trap service, and other maintenance we perform are available for a property's compliance files whenever an inspection requires them.

What's the fastest way to get emergency commercial service?

Call the same 24/7 line as residential emergencies and identify the property as commercial with the nature of the issue, so dispatch can prioritize appropriately for a business that can't operate without water service.

Do you provide a service agreement for ongoing commercial maintenance?

Yes, for a property that wants scheduled inspections, backflow testing, and grease trap service bundled into a recurring agreement rather than booking each visit separately, which also tends to prioritize response time for that property when something urgent comes up.

Can you handle plumbing for a new commercial buildout from start to finish?

Yes, including permitting coordination, rough-in plumbing, and final fixture installation for a new or renovated commercial space.

Do you offer priority response for commercial service agreement customers?

Yes, a property under a standing service agreement generally gets faster scheduling priority than a one-time call, since the relationship is already established.

Can you provide a certificate of insurance for our property records?

Yes, standard for commercial work and available on request as part of the onboarding paperwork for any new commercial account.
