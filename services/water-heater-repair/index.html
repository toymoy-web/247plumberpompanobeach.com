The most common water heater failures we see

No hot water at all usually points to a failed heating element (electric) or a pilot and thermocouple issue (gas), both straightforward same-day fixes. Water that's hot but not hot enough, or that runs out fast, more often means sediment buildup insulating the heating element from the water, which is common in this area's moderately hard water once a tank passes the five-year mark.

A leak at the tank's base is the one that changes the conversation from repair to replacement. Fittings and valves can be repaired. A corroded tank seam itself cannot, and continuing to run a leaking tank risks a much bigger cleanup if it fails completely overnight.

What we check before recommending a fix

Before replacing any part, we check the anode rod, since a depleted anode rod is often the real reason a tank is corroding faster than it should, and replacing it can add years to a tank that would otherwise need full replacement soon. We also check the pressure relief valve, since a valve that's stuck or weeping is a safety issue independent of whatever else brought us out.

For gas units specifically, we check venting and gas supply pressure as part of any repair, not just the component that appears to have failed, since a venting issue can cause symptoms that look like a heater problem but aren't.

What to do if a water heater is actively leaking or making noise

Shut off the cold water supply valve on top of the tank first, then the power source, the breaker for electric units or the gas shutoff valve for gas units, if it's safe to reach. That combination stops the situation from getting worse while waiting for a technician, whether the issue is a leak or a unit that's making a concerning noise.

A rotten-egg smell near a gas water heater is different from a normal repair call. Leave the house and call from outside, then contact the gas utility in addition to a plumber, since that smell can indicate a gas leak rather than a water heater malfunction alone.

Pricing

What Actually Affects Cost

  • Which component failed (element, thermocouple, valve)
  • Unit age and whether parts are still readily available
  • Gas vs electric diagnostic time
  • Whether the tank itself has failed (repair) vs a fitting (replace)

Most homeowner insurance policies cover a sudden, accidental plumbing failure, a burst pipe, for example, but not gradual wear or a line that failed from ordinary age-related corrosion. That's the honest baseline to plan around rather than assume coverage applies.

FAQ

Water Heater Repair Questions

Can you fix a water heater the same day I call?

In most cases yes, since a straightforward element, thermocouple, or valve repair doesn't require special-order parts. A full tank replacement may take a bit longer depending on unit availability, and every repair meets the Florida Building Code, Plumbing standard.

Is it worth repairing a water heater over 10 years old?

Usually not past a second repair. At that age, the tank itself is closer to end of life, and a new repair bill on an old unit rarely makes sense compared to replacement.

My water heater is making a popping or rumbling sound. Is that serious?

It usually means sediment has built up at the bottom of the tank, which reduces efficiency and shortens the tank's life, but it's not an immediate safety issue. Worth having flushed or inspected soon rather than urgently.

Do you repair both gas and electric water heaters?

Yes, both, including diagnosing whether a gas-specific issue like venting or supply pressure is the actual cause.

Can a water heater repair be covered by a home warranty?

Sometimes, depending on the specific plan and whether the failure is considered normal wear versus a covered defect. We can provide documentation of the diagnosis for a warranty claim, though approval decisions are between the homeowner and the warranty company.

Is it normal for a water heater to take longer to heat up as it ages?

Yes, to a point. Sediment buildup insulating the heating element gradually slows recovery time even in a unit with no other problems, which is one reason periodic flushing helps.

Do tankless water heaters ever fail completely and need full replacement?

Less often than a tank, since there is no tank to corrode, but a heat exchanger failure or extensive scale damage on a unit that was never descaled can reach that point. We diagnose which situation a specific unit is actually in before recommending either repair or replacement, and explain the reasoning either way.
