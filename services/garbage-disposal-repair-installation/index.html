The three most common disposal problems

A disposal that hums but doesn't spin usually means the flywheel is jammed, often fixable in minutes with the reset button and the hex key most units include, though a technician can clear a stubborn jam quickly if the homeowner's own attempt didn't work. A disposal that's completely silent when switched on is more often an electrical or motor issue rather than a jam.

A leak at the base of the disposal, rather than at the connections above it, usually means the internal seal has failed, which typically means replacement rather than repair, since that seal isn't something serviced in place.

Choosing a replacement unit

Horsepower and grind stages are the two specs that actually matter for how well a disposal handles a household's real usage, not brand name alone. A single-person household with light use doesn't need the same unit as a family that regularly puts fibrous vegetable scraps down the drain. We size the replacement to actual usage rather than defaulting to a mid-range unit.

Installing a disposal where there wasn't one before

Adding a disposal to a kitchen that never had one means confirming the sink has the right drain configuration, that there's an available electrical circuit or outlet under the sink, and, for a dishwasher already connected to the same drain line, that the disposal's dishwasher inlet gets properly connected rather than left capped. None of this is unusual, but skipping any one of these steps is how a new installation ends up leaking or tripping a breaker within the first week.

For older homes in this coverage area without a dedicated outlet under the sink already, adding one is sometimes part of the job, which we'll flag as part of the estimate rather than as a surprise add-on afterward.

What not to put down a disposal, even a working one

Fibrous vegetable scraps like celery and corn husks, coffee grounds in large quantities, grease and oil, and hard items like bones or fruit pits all shorten a disposal's working life or cause jams even in a properly functioning unit. None of these will break a disposal on the first occurrence, but consistent habits around what goes down the drain make a real difference in how often a unit needs service.

What Actually Affects Cost

  • Repair (jam, reset) vs full replacement
  • Horsepower and grind-stage tier of a new unit
  • Whether a dedicated electrical outlet already exists

Most homeowner insurance policies cover a sudden, accidental plumbing failure, a burst pipe, for example, but not gradual wear or a line that failed from ordinary age-related corrosion. That's the honest baseline to plan around rather than assume coverage applies.

FAQ

Garbage Disposal Repair & Installation Questions

My disposal just hums. Can that be fixed without replacing it?

Often yes. A humming disposal usually means the flywheel is jammed, which is frequently resolved with the reset button and clearing whatever's stuck, not a full replacement.

How long does a disposal replacement take?

Typically under an hour for a straightforward swap using the existing connections.

What size disposal do I actually need?

It depends on household size and how the kitchen actually gets used, not just budget. We can recommend based on actual usage during the visit.

Is it safe to reach into a disposal to clear a jam myself?

No, always switch off power at the breaker first, never reach in with the unit plugged in. A jam is usually clearable from above with a hex key without needing to reach inside at all.

How long do garbage disposals typically last?

Around 8 to 12 years with normal use, though usage habits, what actually goes down the drain, affect that lifespan meaningfully in either direction.

Can a garbage disposal be repaired if parts are no longer available for an old model?

Sometimes generic replacement parts fit, but for a genuinely discontinued model, replacement with a current unit is usually the more practical and cost-effective path forward.

Do all sinks support a garbage disposal installation?

Most standard kitchen sinks do, though a few older or unusually shaped sink configurations may need a minor adjustment, which we'll flag during the estimate.

Is a louder-than-usual disposal a sign of a problem?

Often yes, unusual grinding or rattling can mean something solid is lodged against the blades or a bearing is wearing out, worth having checked before it fails completely.

Is a warranty included with a new disposal installation?

Manufacturer warranty terms vary by model, and we register the installation date so that warranty is properly documented.
