The most common faucet and sink problems

A dripping faucet is almost always a worn cartridge, O-ring, or washer inside the fixture, a straightforward and inexpensive repair on most faucet models. Low pressure at a single faucet, when the rest of the house is normal, usually means a clogged aerator rather than a supply-line issue, which is a quick fix compared to the water-pressure problems that affect a whole house.

A leak under the sink, at the supply lines or the drain trap connections, is worth attention sooner rather than later, since that's exactly the kind of slow leak that causes cabinet and subfloor damage over months before anyone notices standing water. Checking under the sink periodically, even without an obvious problem, catches this early.

Repair or replace a faucet

A faucet under 10 years old with a single failed part is almost always worth repairing rather than replacing. Beyond that age, especially with mineral buildup visible inside the fixture or a discontinued cartridge style, replacement is often the more practical option, and we'll say which situation a specific faucet is in rather than defaulting to either answer.

Choosing a replacement faucet or sink

Finish durability matters more than it might seem for a kitchen faucet specifically, since it sees more daily use than almost any other fixture in the house. A mid-range finish from an established brand generally outlasts a budget faucet with a premium-looking finish that wears within a year or two of regular use.

For a sink replacement, matching the existing countertop cutout saves significant cost compared to modifying the counter itself, which is why we measure the existing opening before recommending options rather than starting from a catalog and working backward.

Bathroom versus kitchen fixture differences

Kitchen faucets see heavier daily use and more exposure to grease and food debris, which is why finish and cartridge durability matter more there than in a bathroom. Bathroom sink faucets more commonly develop mineral buildup from standing water in less frequently used guest bathrooms, a different failure pattern that calls for different maintenance advice depending on which room the fixture is in.

Pricing

What Actually Affects Cost

  • Repair (cartridge, washer) vs full fixture replacement
  • Faucet age and part availability
  • Single fixture vs full sink replacement
  • Supply line or drain trap involvement

Most homeowner insurance policies cover a sudden, accidental plumbing failure, a burst pipe, for example, but not gradual wear or a line that failed from ordinary age-related corrosion. That's the honest baseline to plan around rather than assume coverage applies.

FAQ

Faucet & Sink Repair Questions

Why does my faucet keep dripping after I've tried tightening it?

A drip is almost always a worn internal part, a cartridge, washer, or O-ring, not something fixed by tightening the handle. Replacing that part usually solves it.

Is a leak under my sink urgent?

Worth addressing promptly rather than immediately panicking. A slow supply or drain leak under a sink can cause real cabinet and subfloor damage over time even though it doesn't look dramatic day to day.

Should I repair or replace an old faucet?

Under about 10 years old with one failed part, repair usually makes sense. Older than that, especially with visible mineral buildup, replacement is often more practical.

Can a single faucet issue affect water pressure elsewhere in the house?

Rarely, unless it's a shared supply line issue rather than the faucet itself. Whole-house pressure changes usually point to something upstream of the fixture, worth mentioning when you call.

Do you carry common faucet parts on the truck?

Yes, standard cartridges, O-rings, and washers for common brands are typically on hand, which is why most single-faucet repairs finish in one visit.

Is a leaking supply line under the sink the same repair as a leaking faucet?

No, a supply line leak is at the connection feeding the faucet, not inside the fixture itself, and is usually a quicker fix than an internal faucet cartridge replacement.

Can a faucet repair be done without shutting off water to the whole house?

Yes, isolating the specific fixture's shutoff valves, standard under most sinks, avoids interrupting water to the rest of the home during the repair.

Are all faucet cartridges interchangeable between brands?

No, cartridges are brand and often model-specific, which is why matching the exact part matters more than it might seem for a straightforward-looking repair.

How often should faucet aerators be cleaned?

Roughly once or twice a year in this area's water, more often if flow feels noticeably reduced.
