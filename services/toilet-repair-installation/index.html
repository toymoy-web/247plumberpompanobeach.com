The most common toilet problems

A toilet that runs intermittently or constantly almost always means the flapper valve isn't sealing properly, an inexpensive part that's one of the most common plumbing repairs there is. A weak or incomplete flush more often points to a partially clogged trap or, on an older toilet, mineral buildup restricting the rim jets that push water into the bowl.

Water at the base of the toilet, rather than from the tank, usually means the wax ring seal between the toilet and the drain flange has failed, which requires pulling the toilet to replace, not something fixable from above.

When toilet installation makes more sense than another repair

A toilet older than 15 to 20 years, especially a pre-1994 model using significantly more water per flush than current standards, is often worth replacing outright rather than repairing repeatedly, both for water savings and because parts for very old models can be harder to source. We install to match the existing rough-in measurement so it's a straightforward swap in most standard bathrooms.

Choosing a replacement toilet

Bowl height, flush type, and rough-in measurement (the distance from the wall to the drain center) are the three specs that actually determine fit and comfort, more than brand or price point alone. A comfort-height bowl suits most adults better than a standard-height one, and a dual-flush or pressure-assisted model uses meaningfully less water than an older gravity-flush design without sacrificing performance.

We confirm the existing rough-in measurement before recommending any specific model, since installing the wrong rough-in size turns a straightforward swap into a more involved plumbing modification.

Multiple bathrooms, one at a time or all at once

For a household replacing toilets in more than one bathroom, doing them together often costs less per unit than separate visits, and lets every bathroom match if that matters aesthetically. For a single failing toilet with the rest of the house fine, there's no need to replace fixtures that aren't causing a problem just because one is.

Pricing

What Actually Affects Cost

  • Repair (flapper, fill valve) vs full replacement
  • Rough-in measurement match for a new unit
  • Wax ring reseal if leaking at the base
  • Number of units being serviced at once

Most homeowner insurance policies cover a sudden, accidental plumbing failure, a burst pipe, for example, but not gradual wear or a line that failed from ordinary age-related corrosion. That's the honest baseline to plan around rather than assume coverage applies.

FAQ

Toilet Repair & Installation Questions

Why does my toilet keep running?

Almost always a worn flapper valve not sealing properly against the flush valve seat, an inexpensive and quick repair in most cases.

Is water at the base of my toilet serious?

Yes, worth addressing promptly. It usually means the wax ring seal has failed, and that water can damage the subfloor the longer it continues.

Should I replace a very old toilet even if it still works?

Often worth it for water savings alone if it predates 1994 low-flow standards, on top of parts becoming harder to find for very old models over time.

How much water does a running toilet actually waste?

A continuously running toilet can waste hundreds of gallons a day, which shows up clearly on the next water bill, making it worth fixing promptly even though it isn't an emergency.

Can a toilet be installed in a different spot than the old one?

Moving a toilet's location involves relocating the drain line, a larger plumbing project than a standard swap. We can discuss what that would involve for a specific bathroom layout.

Can a toilet be too low-flow and cause clogging issues?

Older ultra-low-flow models from the early days of the 1994 standard sometimes had this reputation. Modern low-flow toilets have largely solved that with improved bowl design, so this is less of a concern with a current model.

Is it normal for a new toilet installation to need adjustment afterward?

A minor adjustment to the fill valve or flush mechanism shortly after installation is common and included as part of the original installation job, not billed as a separate follow-up call.

Can a toilet be repaired if it's a discontinued or unusual model?

Often yes with a universal-fit replacement part, though a genuinely obsolete model sometimes reaches the point where replacement is more practical than sourcing exact parts.

Can hard water buildup affect a toilet's flush performance over time?

Yes, mineral deposits inside the rim jets and trapway gradually restrict flow, which is a common reason an older toilet flushes noticeably worse than it did when new.
