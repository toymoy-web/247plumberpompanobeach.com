Signs of a sewer line problem worth acting on

Multiple drains backing up at once, a foul smell in the yard over the line's path, unusually green or soggy grass along one stretch of lawn, and gurgling from drains when the washing machine runs are all signs pointing at the main line rather than a single fixture. Any of these on their own is worth investigating. More than one together usually means the problem is active now.

How the repair method gets decided

A camera inspection comes first on nearly every sewer line repair, since it shows whether the damage is a crack or root intrusion suited to trenchless lining, or a collapsed section that needs traditional excavation for that specific stretch. Root intrusion from mature trees is a common cause on older lines in this area, and the fix depends on how much of the pipe roots have actually compromised, not just where they entered.

For a full collapse or a line that's shifted enough to lose proper slope, traditional dig-and-replace is sometimes the more honest answer even though it's more disruptive, and we'll say so rather than push a trenchless option that wouldn't actually hold.

What to do before we arrive for a sewer backup

Avoid running water or flushing toilets until the technician arrives, since additional water can worsen a backup that's already at capacity. If sewage has reached living space, avoid direct contact and keep the area ventilated. Shut off the main water valve if the backup is severe enough that fixtures are overflowing.

Reducing the odds of another sewer backup

Once a line has been repaired, a few habits reduce the odds of another backup: avoiding flushing wipes even ones labeled flushable, keeping grease out of kitchen drains entirely, and having a recurring root intrusion issue jetted on a schedule rather than waiting for the next full backup. None of these guarantee a line never has another issue, but they meaningfully reduce how often the more serious version happens.

For a property with mature trees near the sewer line, a periodic camera check every few years catches root intrusion while it's still a jetting fix rather than letting it progress to a full repair.

What a typical repair timeline looks like

A trenchless repair on a confirmed, accessible section often finishes within a single day once the camera inspection and quote are done. A traditional dig-and-replace repair takes longer, typically one to three days depending on the length of pipe affected and how much restoration the excavation itself requires afterward. We give a realistic timeline once the actual scope is known, not before.

FAQ

Sewer Line Repair Questions

How fast can you respond to a sewer backup?

Dispatch runs 24/7 for sewer backups, since multiple fixtures backing up at once is a genuine emergency, not something to wait on.

Will you need to dig up my yard?

Not necessarily. A camera inspection determines whether trenchless lining works for the specific damage, which avoids a full dig in many cases, though a full collapse sometimes still needs traditional excavation.

Are tree roots really a common cause of sewer line damage here?

Yes, particularly on older lines under mature landscaping, which describes a meaningful share of properties in this part of Broward County. Repairs follow the same Florida Building Code, Plumbing standard applied across every job on this site.

Can a sewer line be repaired without affecting my landscaping?

Often yes, if trenchless lining is a fit for the specific damage. A traditional dig-and-replace repair does affect the ground above the repaired section, which we'll explain clearly before work starts.

How much warning do sewer lines usually give before a full backup?

Sometimes weeks, in the form of slow drains or occasional gurgling. Sometimes very little, particularly with a sudden collapse. That variability is part of why acting on early signs matters.

Is a sewer line repair typically covered by homeowners insurance?

It depends on the cause and the specific policy. Sudden collapse from an external event is more likely to be considered than gradual deterioration from age, which most policies treat as a maintenance issue rather than a covered loss.

Can a partial sewer line collapse be temporarily bypassed until full repair?

In some cases a temporary bypass line keeps water flowing while parts or scheduling for the full repair are arranged, though this is a short-term measure, not a fix.

Do you offer any kind of guarantee on sewer line repairs?

Workmanship on the repair itself is stood behind for a reasonable period. We can discuss specific terms directly since they depend on the repair method used.
