Preparing Your Commercial Kitchen Plumbing for Inspection Day

A health or plumbing inspection can catch a commercial kitchen off guard if nothing has been checked ahead of time. Here's what actually gets checked during a typical visit, and how to properly prepare well before the inspector ever arrives on site.

By the Pompano Beach 24/7 Plumbing team · Updated 2026

What to do before an inspector arrives on inspection day

Run water through every floor and kitchen drain the morning of the inspection to confirm normal flow, have all compliance documentation physically accessible rather than needing to search for it, and address any known issue, however minor, rather than hoping it goes unnoticed.

What inspectors actually look for

Grease trap condition and maintenance records, backflow prevention device certification, floor drain function, and general drain line condition are the most common plumbing-related items on a commercial kitchen inspection. Broward County and most Florida municipalities require documentation for grease trap maintenance and backflow testing specifically, not just a visual pass, so having records on hand matters as much as the equipment itself being in working order.

Grease trap compliance, the most common failure point

An overfull or poorly maintained grease trap is one of the most common reasons a commercial kitchen fails a plumbing-related inspection. The common industry standard is cleaning once a trap is about 25% full by volume, not a fixed monthly or quarterly calendar interval regardless of actual use, since a high-volume kitchen fills a trap faster than a lower-volume one. Keeping documented cleaning records, not just doing the cleaning, is what actually satisfies most inspection requirements.

Backflow prevention testing

Most commercial properties in Florida are required to have a tested backflow prevention device, typically on an annual basis, which keeps contaminated water from siphoning back into the public supply. Inspectors generally want to see current certification, not just a device physically present, so scheduling this testing on a reliable annual cycle avoids a last-minute scramble before an inspection.

Floor drains and general drain condition

Floor drains that are slow, clogged, or emit odor are a common and visible inspection issue, often traceable to grease or debris buildup rather than a structural problem. A general drain cleaning pass ahead of a scheduled inspection, particularly for kitchen and floor drains, addresses this proactively rather than risking a finding during the actual inspection.

Building a simple pre-inspection checklist

Confirm grease trap cleaning records are current and accessible, verify backflow prevention certification hasn't lapsed, run water through floor drains to check for slow drainage, and address any known plumbing issues, however minor, before the inspection date rather than after. None of this needs to happen the day before. Building it into a regular maintenance schedule means inspection prep is largely already done by the time an inspector actually shows up.

Keeping documentation organized between inspections

A simple binder or digital folder with grease trap cleaning receipts, backflow testing certificates, and any plumbing repair invoices, organized by date, turns an inspection from a scramble into a five-minute review. Many properties find that once this system exists, maintaining it going forward takes minimal effort compared to the value of never being caught without documentation an inspector asks for.

What happens during a follow-up inspection after a violation

If an initial inspection finds a violation, a follow-up inspection typically gets scheduled within a set window, often 30 to 60 days depending on the severity and the specific municipality's process. Addressing the finding promptly and having documentation ready for the follow-up, showing the specific corrective action taken, meaningfully improves the odds of a clean pass on the second visit rather than facing an extended compliance timeline.

FAQ

Commercial Inspection Prep Questions

How far ahead should grease trap cleaning be scheduled before an inspection?

Ideally, grease trap maintenance should already be on a regular, ongoing schedule well before any inspection is ever announced, rather than being scheduled reactively once a specific date becomes known.

What happens if backflow certification has lapsed?

This is typically flagged as a violation requiring prompt retesting. Keeping the annual testing cycle current avoids this becoming an inspection-day surprise.

Can a failed inspection affect a restaurant's ability to operate?

Depending on the severity of the finding, yes, ranging from a required follow-up inspection to a more serious operational restriction, which is exactly why proactive ongoing maintenance matters so much.

Do you provide documentation we can show an inspector directly?

Yes, records of grease trap cleaning, backflow testing, and any other maintenance performed are available for a property's compliance files.

Does a small food truck need the same plumbing compliance as a full restaurant?

Requirements vary by jurisdiction and setup, but grease and wastewater handling compliance often applies in some form even to smaller mobile food operations, worth confirming with local health officials.

Can plumbing inspection findings affect a business license renewal?

In some jurisdictions, unresolved health or plumbing code violations can genuinely affect a business license renewal process, which is one more solid reason proactive compliance is always worth the ongoing effort involved.

How long does a typical commercial plumbing inspection take?

Often 30 minutes to an hour depending on the property's size and complexity, though a follow-up inspection after a violation may be more focused and shorter.

Are floor drain traps required to hold water at all times?

Yes, a dry floor drain trap allows sewer gas to enter the space, which is both an odor issue and a code violation, so periodically running water down infrequently used floor drains is worth doing.

Can a restaurant fail inspection for a plumbing issue unrelated to grease?

Yes, any of the plumbing-related items covered here, backflow certification, floor drains, general drain condition, can independently cause a finding regardless of grease trap status.

Does a commercial kitchen need a separate grease trap for each sink?

Not necessarily each sink individually, but the overall grease-generating fixtures typically need to drain through a properly sized trap system, sized during initial kitchen design or a later retrofit.
