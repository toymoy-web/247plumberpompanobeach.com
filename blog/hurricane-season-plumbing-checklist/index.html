Storm Prep

Hurricane Season Plumbing Checklist for South Florida Homes

A few plumbing checks before the season's first storm can prevent the kind of flooding and water damage that catches homeowners off guard when it matters most.

By the Pompano Beach 24/7 Plumbing team · Updated 2026

What to do if flooding starts during a storm

Move electronics and valuables off the floor immediately, avoid contact with standing water near any electrical outlet or appliance, and shut off the main water valve if a plumbing failure, not just rainwater, appears to be contributing to the water. Call for help once it's safe to do so rather than waiting for the storm to fully pass.

Why plumbing prep matters as much as storm shutters

Most hurricane prep checklists focus on windows, generators, and food supplies, and skip plumbing almost entirely. That's a real gap, since heavy rain and power outages, the two most common effects of a tropical storm or hurricane in this area, both directly affect a home's plumbing systems in ways that cause real damage if nothing was checked beforehand.

Test the sump pump before the season starts, not during the first storm

For any property with a sump pump, pour a few gallons of water into the pit and confirm it activates, runs smoothly, and shuts off correctly. This takes a few minutes and catches most failures before they matter. A battery backup system deserves the same check, since the same storms that trigger heavy rain are also the ones most likely to cause a power outage, exactly when a pump that only runs on household power stops working.

Know where the main shutoff valve is, and confirm it works

Every household should know the location of the main water shutoff valve before an emergency, not while water is actively flowing somewhere it shouldn't. Turn the valve to confirm it moves freely. A valve that's stuck or hard to turn from age is worth having serviced now rather than discovering that during an actual crisis.

Check outdoor plumbing and drainage before heavy rain arrives

Clear debris from any exterior drains or French drains, and check that downspouts direct water away from the foundation rather than pooling against it. For a property with a septic system, avoid heavy water use right before a predicted storm, since saturated ground during hurricane season can slow drain field absorption and make an already-full tank's symptoms worse.

After the storm: what to check before assuming everything is fine

Once a storm passes, check for any new dampness, unusual odors, or standing water around the foundation, sump pit, or under sinks, even if nothing was visibly damaged during the storm itself. A slow leak that started during the storm's stress on the plumbing system doesn't always show up immediately, and catching it in the first day or two afterward keeps a small issue from becoming a bigger one.

Building a simple hurricane season plumbing kit

A few inexpensive items make a real difference if plumbing does become part of storm response: a wrench sized to the main shutoff valve if it's not a lever-style handle, a battery-powered flashlight kept near the water heater and sump pit for after-dark checks during an outage, and a written note of the main shutoff's exact location for anyone in the household who might need to find it under stress.

None of this replaces professional help when something does go wrong, but having these basics ready means less scrambling during the exact moment when calm, quick action matters most.

Coordinating with neighbors on shared concerns

In neighborhoods with older, shared-era plumbing, a conversation with neighbors about sump pump status, generator availability, or shared drainage concerns before storm season starts can surface useful information, particularly in a low-lying area where multiple properties may be affected by the same drainage patterns during heavy rain. This isn't a substitute for individual preparation, but it adds context that a single household's checklist alone might miss.

FAQ

Hurricane Prep Questions

Do all homes in this area need a sump pump?

No, only properties with a genuine history of water intrusion in a low-lying yard or below-grade space. A property with good drainage and no history of flooding typically doesn't need one added as a precaution alone.

Should I turn off my main water valve before evacuating?

For an extended evacuation, this is a reasonable precaution that prevents a burst pipe or fixture failure from flooding an empty house while no one is there to notice.

How often should sump pump batteries be checked?

At the start of hurricane season at minimum, and again mid-season if the area sees an active storm pattern, since battery backups degrade over time even without being used.

Can heavy rain alone cause plumbing problems without flooding?

Yes, saturated ground can affect septic drain field absorption and put extra pressure on outdoor drainage systems, even without any water actually entering the home.

Should outdoor drains be covered before a storm?

No, covering drains can cause worse pooling around the foundation. Keeping them clear of debris so they function normally is more effective than blocking them.

Is it worth having a plumber do a pre-season inspection?

For a property with any history of drainage or sump pump issues, yes, a professional check catches problems a homeowner's own test might miss.

Should I disconnect outdoor hoses before a storm?

Yes, disconnecting hoses and covering outdoor spigots reduces the chance of wind-driven debris damaging connections during high winds.

Does a generator need to be sized for a sump pump specifically?

If a sump pump is a priority during an outage, confirming the generator's capacity includes the pump's startup power draw, often higher than its running wattage, is worth checking beforehand.

How long can drying equipment safely run unattended?

Professional-grade equipment is designed for continuous unattended operation over several days if needed, monitored periodically rather than requiring someone present at all times.

Should storm shutters cover outdoor plumbing fixtures too?

Standard shutters are designed for windows, but similarly securing or covering exposed outdoor plumbing during a major storm can reduce debris-related damage to fittings.
